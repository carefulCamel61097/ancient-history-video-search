12,000-year-old Elongated Skull in China

Tina · 18 min · watch on YouTube · summarised 2026-09-27

cross-continentaldatingconvergencewhycranial-deformationancient-dnashared-symbolslost-civilisation-hypothesis

The find 00:00–02:36. At Houtaomuga, on the south-western edge of the Amur River basin in north-east China, a site with Neolithic burials spanning 12,000 to 5,000 years ago produced the remains of 25 individuals. Put through a CT scanner, 11 showed indisputable signs of skull shaping — flattened and elongated foreheads. They are male and female, from toddlers to adults, and the oldest, an adult male about 12,000 years old, makes this the earliest intentional cranial modification known on the Eurasian continent.

The tradition at that one site ran for 7,000 years.

The 12,000-year-old man is numbered M45, and his maternal DNA is D4h1, a lineage found in North Asians, Central Asians and American Indians. A 2020 paper proposes the Houtaomuga people as Paleo-Siberians who may have contributed to the first American lineage.

A second deformed skull, carbon dated to about 11,000 years, was pulled from the bed of the Songhua River, the Amur's longest tributary.

The find the video rests on: eleven shaped skulls from one site, the oldest about 12,000 years old.

How and who 02:37–03:30. The shape can only be altered in infancy, while the skull is soft, so the practice required knowledge and care — and studies have found it causes no neurological impairment. At Houtaomuga the people with shaped skulls were buried with more offerings, including shell decorations, and that includes young children, which suggests inherited status rather than earned. The reading she reports is that head shaping marked privilege.

How widespread it is

03:30–08:29 Every inhabited continent. Steppe cultures including the Huns and the Alans 1,500 years ago; researchers think it was common in Egypt around Akhenaten and Tutankhamun; and in modern times among Oceanic and African peoples.

regionwhat she gives
Middle East"common in the Near East during the Neolithic and Chalcolithic", with examples back 11,000 years; finds across Iraq, south-western Iran, Jordan, Lebanon, Palestine, eastern Turkey, the Zagros valleys and the western Persian Gulf shore, dated 7500 to 4000 BC
AustraliaKow Swamp, Nacurrie and Coobool Creek, some 11,000 to 12,000 years old and confirmed as artificially deformed. Aboriginal Australian and Melanesian lineages fall in mitochondrial branches M and N and Y-chromosome founders C and F
South Americaearliest in Peru at 7000–6000 BC; Argentina and Chile too, the oldest about 8,000 years from the Argentinean Pampas; later the Maya, the Inca and some North American peoples

So three regions — north-east China, the Middle East and Australia — reach back to roughly the same date, and South America starts about two thousand years later. Her question is whether that can be coincidence.

What are the odds of such a phenomenon springing up like this all over the planet with no connection or common stimulus? I think highly unlikely; unless they were all inspired by some similar encounters, perhaps from an extraterrestrial source. However, here I'm only going to discuss earthly reasoning.

The Pacific route

08:29–12:09 For South America she proposes arrival by sea.

  • Population Y, a proposed 'ghost population' that may have been in South America 16,000 years ago, shows up as extra ancestry in a few present-day Amazonian groups — and it is more closely related to Australian Aboriginal and Melanesian people than to the Siberian ancestors of most Native Americans.
  • Darrell "Dusty" Crawford, a member of the Blackfeet tribe, had his DNA tested in 2019; on a preliminary analysis his ancestors were in the Americas about 17,000 years ago and had come from the Pacific Islands, reaching the South American coast and moving north.

Then the geography. Seventeen thousand years ago, during the Oldest Dryas, sea level stood roughly 110 to 120 m lower than today. New Guinea was joined to Australia, the Pacific islands were much larger, and some now submerged. On a map of the Pacific sea floor she traces a line of islands running from Australia to Peru, and argues they would have served as stopping and resupplying points. The Younger Dryas, 12,900 to 11,700 years ago, gives a second low-sea window for a second wave.

The sea level her Pacific route depends on.

The map she traces the route on is a bathymetric relief map with no depth scale, no contours and no figures. The "group of islands forming a dotted line" is the Pacific's seamount chains drawn in shaded relief — submarine mountains, rendered the same way whether their summits lie 50 m or 5,000 m down. Nothing on the image distinguishes the two.

The route: a chain of islands she traces from Australia to Peru, which is the whole of the evidence offered for a Pacific crossing.

For North America she uses the other route: M45's maternal lineage is found in North Eurasians and American Indians, and ancient North Eurasians are known to have crossed Beringia, so the tradition could have travelled that way too.

Where it started

12:09–15:12 Her argument for a single origin is an analogy: rather than modern humans evolving independently in pockets around the world, they came out of Africa and populated it — and head binding could have spread the same way.

If so, the source should be one of the three oldest regions, and it must have been a place people migrated from. The Middle East is her answer, because it was the first stop out of Africa and almost everyone passed through it, and because there is evidence of movement from there to Australia, Siberia and north-east China but no data for reverse migration in that period.

The question at the end

15:12–17:51 Why an elongated head, specifically?

Not to imitate Neanderthals — theirs are long front to back rather than tall, and most primates have elongated skulls anyway. Not to look taller, since height was not a marker of rank. Her preferred answer is emulation: people were copying an elite who naturally had that head shape.

So she goes looking for representations, and finishes on three sets of figurines:

  • Yarmukian clay figures from Israel, over 8,000 years old, with long pointed heads
  • a mother goddess figure from Tell es-Sawwan in Iraq, the same era
  • and the Ubaid figures of Mesopotamia, 6500–3800 BC — female figures with what she describes as lizard faces, long pointed heads and slender bodies, some suckling babies whose heads are the same shape, and a male figure with a tall long head

The Ubaid period precedes the Sumerian city states, and she notes that Sumerians had rounded heads and did not bind, which their art shows.

Were these figures made to represent these ancient people or the gods they worshipped? Did their gods have elongated skulls? I don't know but I'm intrigued by the possibility.

Where the video ends up: figurines with long pointed heads from before the Sumerian city states.

What you can check yourself

  • Houtaomuga is an excavated, published site. Twenty-five individuals, eleven with confirmed modification, CT-scanned, with a date range — all of it in the literature.
  • M45's mitochondrial haplogroup, D4h1, is published, and where that lineage is found is a matter of record.
  • The Australian sites are named — Kow Swamp, Nacurrie, Coobool Creek — and both their dates and the artificial-deformation diagnosis are argued about in print.
  • The Middle Eastern range, 7500 to 4000 BC, is a stated span across named modern countries.
  • Population Y is a real proposal in the genetics literature and can be read, including the objections to it.
  • Sea level at the Last Glacial Maximum, about 120 m below present, is standard and mapped.
  • The Ubaid and Yarmukian figurines are in museums and published catalogues. What they look like is not in dispute; what they mean is.
  • That Sumerian art shows rounded heads is checkable against any collection of Sumerian sculpture.
  • Whether head binding causes neurological harm has been studied and the answer is published.

What the video does not settle

  • "What are the odds" is the argument. Independent invention of head binding is treated as implausible, and nothing is offered on how easily the practice might be arrived at separately.
  • Her own haplogroups are described as near-universal outside Africa. Mitochondrial M and N and Y-chromosome C and F are, in her words, "enormous haplogroups that are found in ancient populations on every continent except Africa", and they are then used to link Australia to a particular place.
  • The Crawford result is a commercial DNA test reported in the news. She calls it "further evidence proving ancient humans migrated across the vast Pacific", and describes it in the same sentence as a preliminary analysis. No publication is named.
  • The island chain has to have been above water, and no depth is given anywhere. Her own sea-level figure is on screen: a published curve with four labelled meltwater pulses, on which her marker sits at about −107 m rather than the 110–120 m she states beside it. How deep the features on her route lie is not given, and the map she uses cannot say which of them were dry land.
  • The Middle East conclusion rests on an absence. "No data signifying reverse migration" is offered as the reason to prefer one of three candidates, and it assumes both that the record is complete and that the three oldest finds are the three oldest that existed.
  • Population Y is about a deep ancestry signal, not a documented voyage. That some Amazonian groups carry an Australasian-related component is the finding; a Pacific crossing is one proposed explanation among several, and the alternatives are not mentioned.
  • The figurines are described, not interpreted. "Lizard faces" is her own phrase for the convention, and the step from a pointed clay head to a population with naturally elongated skulls is not made — she puts it as a question.
  • Akhenaten and Tutankhamun are cited as "researchers think" with no source given.
  • Brien Foerster is named for the Peruvian skulls as a reference point for viewers; the claims associated with that material are not examined here.
