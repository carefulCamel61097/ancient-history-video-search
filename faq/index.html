How an answer here is written

A dismissal is rephrased as a question. Someone says "obviously that's to be earthquake resistant"; the entry asks "Aren't the walls built that way to resist earthquakes?" An assertion answered by an assertion is a fight, and the reader picks a side by temperament. A question answered with evidence is not.

The question is written to be the strongest form of the objection, not the version we happened to encounter. Where an objection has a serious version and a lazy one, the entry answers the serious one. If the answer only works against the lazy version, the entry is not ready. No source quote is printed: a quote from one person's feed looks like an external check without being one.

Every answer has the same two fields. What is usually said — the standard account, stated fairly and without sneering. Where that stops — what it covers, what it leaves, and whether it was ever tested. A proposed mechanism is not an explanation until it excludes the alternatives and covers the site's other features.

The usual answer is that we do not know. "We don't ultimately know, but this is the information we have and this is the speculation that it leads some people to." That is the normal shape of an entry here, not a fallback for hard cases.

Sometimes the dismissal is right, and the entry says so. The corpus already carries claims the alternative side examined and dropped. An FAQ where every answer is a rebuttal has stopped being an FAQ.

Claims stay attributed. The site's own voice states only what a proposed explanation fails to cover. Positive claims about what did happen belong to the presenter who made them, by name, with the entry and timestamp.

Every answer carries the date it was last reviewed. Video entries record what one video argued on one date and never need revising. An answer here can be overturned by a better mainstream account, by a presenter's evidence being deflated, or by a new entry.
